# German-law analysis

Build the answer around the legally relevant dispute rather than a generic list
of doctrines.

1. Identify the parties, requested result, relevant date, and procedural posture.
   Separate claims against each potential debtor and work chronologically where
   timing changes the legal result.
2. Select plausible claim bases and prioritize the ones that can actually decide
   the matter. Do not treat a citation edge or keyword hit as applicability.
3. For each serious candidate, make a compact working matrix:
   - legal basis;
   - constituent requirements;
   - supporting and opposing facts;
   - retrieved authority for disputed requirements;
   - missing fact or evidence;
   - provisional result.
4. Apply the facts to each material requirement. Distinguish settled doctrine,
   competing views, factual uncertainty, and the agent's own inference. Do not
   hide a missing contractual, procedural, or evidentiary predicate inside a
   confident conclusion.
5. Test defenses, exclusions, limitation periods, burden-of-proof issues, and
   the strongest reasonable counterargument only where they can change the
   outcome.

Ask a focused follow-up when one missing fact controls the branch. Otherwise
state both branches or make an explicit limited assumption. Use primary text
from RechtsGraph and verify current status, contested interpretation, and case
law through actual web research when material.

Present the likely result first, then the decisive reasoning by claim. Use a
full Gutachtenstil only when requested or useful; otherwise keep the subsumption
visible without ceremonial headings. Give confidence per contested issue, not
as an invented numerical probability.
