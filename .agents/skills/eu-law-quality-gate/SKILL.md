# EU-law quality gate

Before relying on an EU instrument, verify the parts of this checklist that can
change the answer:

- Identity: official title, act type, number, CELEX identifier, language, and
  whether the retrieved text is original, amended, or consolidated.
- Time: entry into force, application date, transitional provisions, amendments,
  repeal, and the version relevant to the facts. A consolidation date alone is
  not a validity determination.
- Scope: territorial, personal, and material scope plus definitions and explicit
  exclusions.
- Legal effect: distinguish regulations, directives, decisions, delegated acts,
  implementing acts, recitals, and non-binding guidance. For a directive,
  identify the implementation deadline and relevant national measures before
  discussing direct effect or conforming interpretation.
- Hierarchy and interaction: check the relationship to national law, lex
  specialis provisions, implementing rules, and cross-referenced EU acts.
- Fundamental rights: analyze Charter applicability, including Article 51, only
  when the situation falls within EU-law scope.
- Authority: use EUR-Lex or another official EU text for legislation and CURIA
  for judgments where possible. Verify case number, court, date, operative part,
  and the relevant grounds; do not infer doctrine from a search snippet.

Use RechtsGraph to retrieve local EU text and structure efficiently, then use
live official sources for material currency and interpretation checks. Record a
precise evidence gap if the authoritative text or judgment cannot be obtained.

In the answer, state which version and date were checked. Separate the EU rule,
any national implementation, and the application to the facts. Do not present a
directive as if it automatically operated like a regulation.
