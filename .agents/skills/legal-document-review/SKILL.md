# Legal document review

Review from the user's actual role and purpose, not from a generic checklist.

Establish the document type, parties, governing law or jurisdiction, relevant
date, user side, intended transaction or action, and any supplied playbook. Ask
only for missing information that would materially change the review. Never
invent the user's preferred risk thresholds, fallback positions, or commercial
terms.

Read the complete relevant document, including definitions, schedules, tables,
cross-references, amendment clauses, and signature or notice mechanics. For each
finding provide:

- exact clause, passage, or identifiable omission;
- what the document presently does;
- concrete legal or operational consequence for the user's side;
- severity tied to that consequence;
- recommended action or revision-ready wording where requested;
- unresolved fact or authority needed to confirm it.

Check internal consistency before external law. Then research only the legal
rules that matter to a finding, using current primary sources. Distinguish a
document defect from a commercially unfavorable but valid allocation of risk.

Actively control false positives: do not reward the review for producing more
issues. Preserve deliberately acceptable language, state when a common clause
is not problematic on the available facts, and avoid claiming invalidity or
unenforceability without adequate authority. Rank findings so real blockers are
not buried among drafting preferences.

For comparisons, use the same issue taxonomy for every document and separate
textual differences from legal significance. For revisions, preserve the
original meaning and formatting outside the requested changes and mark factual
placeholders instead of fabricating names, dates, amounts, or approvals.
